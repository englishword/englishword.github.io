**Sound-form annotation (IPS notation)** is a kind of direct inline phonetic notation: without changing the spelling of a word, it distinguishes different readings of a word by marking symbols above the word's vowel letters and a few consonant letters. It is more concise than IPA symbols and other respelling symbols.

The IPSM Approach divides a word's main vowel pronunciation, from "strong" to "weak," into four rhythms: open syllable, closed syllable, light syllable, and weak syllable. The open syllable is marked with a horizontal line (─), the closed syllable is unmarked (∅), the light syllable is marked with a dot (⦁), and the weak syllable is marked with a ring (○).

IPS notation and IPA correspond one-to-one: a word's IPA can be converted into IPS, and vice versa. If the reader has not yet mastered IPA, this does not affect the learning of IPS; if the reader has already mastered IPA, it not only helps in learning IPS but also leads to a better understanding of IPA.

Compared with IPA, IPS is created according to the pronunciation rules of words and is concise and clear; through its subtle influence one masters the pronunciation rules of words, so that phonetic annotation is no longer needed.

A second-grade primary school student who has mastered the 26 English letters and Chinese pinyin can learn IPS notation.

## Vowel Letter + R

### Open Sound + R

| Open sound + R | [ ār ] = [ er ] | [ ēr ] = [ ir ] | [ īr ] | [ ōr ] = [ or ] | [ ūr ] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | cāre | hēre | fīre | fōre | dūre |

*Note: when an R sound follows immediately after an open sound, since the R sound involves a retroflex curl, the open sound changes naturally. IPS annotates the sound before the change, while IPA records the sound after the change; the two produce the same result.*

| Closed sound + R | [ er ] | [ or ] |

## Common Suffix Pronunciations

The pronunciation rules for -ed and -s/-es are fixed; although this book gives distinct annotations, they may, for simplicity, be left unmarked.

Some other suffixes, such as -ly, -ous, -ity, etc., are pronounced according to the rules; although this book annotates them all, since their pronunciation is uniform, they may for simplicity be left unmarked.

### The -ed Suffix

| -ed pronunciation rule | Example |
|---|---|
| After voiced consonants and vowels [𝕖d] ⟹ [d] | call𝕖d, borrow𝕖d, mov𝕖d |
| After voiceless consonants [𝕖ḋ] ⟹ [t] | ask𝕖ḋ, finish𝕖ḋ, help𝕖ḋ |
| After the [t] sound [ėd] ⟹ [id] | wantėd, startėd |
| After the [d] sound [ėd] ⟹ [id] | needėd, countėd |

### The -s/-es Suffix

| | -s(-es) pronunciation rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| After consonants | after [s], [z], [sh], [s̃], [ch], [j] sounds add -es, pronounced [ėṡ] ⟹ [iz] | glassėṡ, buzzėṡ, washėṡ, teachėṡ |
| | after voiceless consonants add s, pronounced [s] ⟹ [s] | books, typ𝕖s |
| | after voiced consonants add s, pronounced [ṡ] ⟹ [z] | bagṡ, lin𝕖ṡ, besid𝕖ṡ |
| After vowels | after the letter o add -es, pronounced [𝕖ṡ] ⟹ [z] | tomato𝕖ṡ, potato𝕖ṡ, do𝕖ṡ |
| | change -y to i and add -es, pronounced [𝕖ṡ] ⟹ [z] | ladi𝕖ṡ, fli𝕖ṡ |
| | otherwise pronounced [ṡ] ⟹ [z] | newṡ, dayṡ, boyṡ, beeṡ |
